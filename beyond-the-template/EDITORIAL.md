# Editorial rules

These are proposed defaults. Each one is a decision for the editor to confirm or change before the first entry publishes.

## What gets in
- Root cause is public from a primary source (the company, a regulator, or the incident responder).
- At least three steps of the attack path can be mapped to criteria.
- The incident teaches something about control design. Volume of records lost is not a reason on its own.

## When it can publish
- Not before the company's own account exists. Early reporting opens a candidate and nothing more.
- Two people have signed: a mapper and a different reviewer.

## What an entry may claim
- `event` text states only what a cited source says.
- Inference lives in `rationale` and is worded as inference.
- No entry states or implies what a company's SOC II report, scope or controls contained unless the report is cited.
- Template wording is generic. It is never attributed to a named vendor or auditor.
- Custom controls are written about the environment type, not as instructions to the named company.

## Corrections
- Every change to a published entry adds a dated changelog line saying what changed and why.
- A factual error raised by the company named is answered within two business days.

## After publishing
- Entries are watched for 90 days. New facts open an update issue.
- After 90 quiet days the status moves to closed.

## House style
- Write "SOC II" rather than "SOC 2", since that is the house spelling across this site and its other articles.
- Avoid closing a paragraph with a pair of short, punchy sentences or setting up a point in one clipped sentence and paying it off in the next, because that cadence reads as machine-written; carry the reasoning through connected sentences instead.
